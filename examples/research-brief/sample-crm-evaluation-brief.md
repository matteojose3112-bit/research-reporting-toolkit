# Sample Research Brief: Evaluating a CRM for a Small Service Business

> **Portfolio demonstration only.** This is a reusable decision framework, not a live vendor comparison or a claim about any specific CRM product. Replace the criteria and collect current, cited evidence before making a real recommendation.

**Audience:** Owner or operations lead at a small service business  
**Question:** How should a small service business compare CRM options before choosing one?  
**Scope:** Evaluation process and decision criteria; no vendor is ranked in this sample.

## Executive takeaway

Choose a CRM by testing how well it supports the business's real customer workflow—not by feature count alone. Define the must-have workflow, compare vendors against the same criteria, verify total cost and integration requirements, and run a small pilot using realistic scenarios before committing.

## 1. Decision context

A service business may need to capture inquiries, track follow-ups, schedule or hand off work, maintain customer history, and report on lead conversion. The actual needs depend on team size, existing tools, and whether the company manages appointments, field technicians, or recurring service.

These statements describe the evaluation context; they are not a vendor-specific market finding.

## 2. Suggested evaluation criteria

| Criterion | Questions to verify | Suggested weight |
|---|---|---:|
| Lead and contact management | Can staff see ownership, status, contact history, and next action? | 20% |
| Workflow fit | Can it represent the current inquiry-to-booking process without awkward workarounds? | 20% |
| Ease of use | Can a new team member complete common tasks with minimal training? | 15% |
| Reporting | Can managers track lead source, response time, conversion, and workload? | 15% |
| Integrations | Does it connect to the tools the business already uses? | 10% |
| Total cost | What are subscription, onboarding, seat, usage, and add-on costs? | 10% |
| Data access and portability | Can the business export its records in a usable format? | 5% |
| Support and security | Are support options, permissions, and data-handling terms suitable? | 5% |

**Weighting note:** These weights are illustrative starting points. The buyer should adjust them before scoring vendors.

## 3. Fair comparison method

1. List must-have requirements and deal-breakers before reviewing vendors.
2. Select two to four options that plausibly fit the business.
3. Use official product documentation, pricing pages, and vendor answers as evidence.
4. Record the date checked and link each claim to a source ID.
5. Mark missing information as “Not verified” rather than filling gaps with assumptions.
6. Score each criterion on the same scale (for example, 1–5) and explain each score.
7. Test the finalists using identical scenarios and sample data.

### Example pilot scenarios
- Capture a new lead and assign an owner.
- Record a call and schedule a follow-up.
- Move a lead through each stage of the actual pipeline.
- Find the full history of an existing customer.
- Produce a weekly lead-source and conversion report.
- Export a small set of records for review.

## 5. Recommendation

Use a short requirements checklist and a weighted comparison, then pilot the strongest one or two options with real workflows and non-sensitive sample data. The best fit is the option that meets the must-haves, is usable by the team, integrates with existing systems, and has a clear total cost—not necessarily the option with the longest feature list.

## 6. Limitations and next steps

- No live vendor research was conducted for this demonstration.
- Product capabilities, prices, and terms change; verify them directly before deciding.
- The criteria and weights are examples and should be adapted to the buyer's workflow.
